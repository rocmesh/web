Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32S3 & Wio-SX1262

A tiny, low-cost LoRa node in a ready-made case that makes a great always-on home base for MeshCore or Meshtastic, for about the price of a pizza.

MeshCore Meshtastic Home base Wi-Fi ~$20-28

What it is

This kit from Seeed Studio pairs two thumbnail-sized boards: the XIAO ESP32S3, a small computer with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, and the Wio-SX1262, a LoRa radio that plugs on top of it. Together they make a complete mesh node, and you use the MeshCore or Meshtastic app to send and read messages over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi.

It comes in a 3D-printed case with an external 2 dBi antenna and a USB-C cable, so there's no soldering. There's no screen, GPS or battery, which keeps it cheap. It's at its best plugged into USB power somewhere in your home.

Role in the mesh

Recommended

Home base companion

What it does best. Plug it into a USB charger near a window and leave it on. Connect from your phone or computer over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi whenever you're home.

Works

Repeater

Fine as an indoor repeater on USB power. The ESP32 uses too much power for a small solar setup, though. For an outdoor site, see the Basic Repeater.

Not recommended

Pocket companion

There's no battery included, and the ESP32 drains one much faster than nRF52 boards. To carry one with you, get a T114 or T1000-E instead.

Where to buy and what it costs

Buy the kit with the 3D-printed enclosure. Seeed sells the same boards without it, and the XIAO nRF52840 version uses a different Wio-SX1262 board, so make sure the listing says ESP32S3.

The build

Assembly notes

  1. Flash the firmware before you close up the case (see Setup below). The kit may arrive with gateway firmware rather than MeshCore or Meshtastic.
  2. If the boards aren't already joined, press the Wio-SX1262 onto the XIAO's board-to-board connector.
  3. Connect the antenna and screw it on. Never power the radio without an antenna attached.
  4. Close the case and plug it into USB power.
