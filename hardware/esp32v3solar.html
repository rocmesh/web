What it is

This is our budget solar repeater. A Heltec V3 and two Samsung 18650 batteries sit inside a clear, water-resistant storage box from Harbor Freight. Two small solar panels face up through the clear lid, and an outdoor antenna mounts through the side of the box.

The ESP32 in the V3 uses more power than nRF52 boards, so this build leans on two batteries to get through the night and cloudy days. Give it a spot with plenty of sun. For a repeater that's easier to keep running all year, see the RAK 1W Repeater.

Where to buy and what it costs

There's no kit for this build. Everything comes from the parts list below. Make sure the Heltec V3 is the 915 MHz version.

The build

The V3, the battery holder and a small solar charge board sit in the bottom of the box, with the panels in the lid and the antenna mounted through the side. The charge board sits between the panels and the batteries so they can't be overcharged.

Assembly notes

  1. Flash the firmware first (see Setup below).
  2. Mount the antenna through the side of the box and connect its cable to the V3. Never power the radio without an antenna attached.
  3. Wire the solar panels to the charge board's solar input, and the charge board's battery output to the 18650 holder. Don't wire the panels straight to the batteries: nothing would stop them overcharging, which can damage the cells or start a fire.
  4. Wire the battery holder to the V3's battery connector. Double-check polarity before connecting.
  5. Fix the panels inside the lid facing out, tuck the wiring in, and close the box so the seal sits flat.

Setup
