Basic Repeater

A $15 solar spotlight from Lowe's, rebuilt around a RAK board and an Alfa antenna. It charges itself from its own panel, so you can mount it and forget it.

MeshCore Meshtastic Repeater Low power Solar ~$97

What it is

The Basic Repeater starts as a Harbor Breeze solar spotlight. The solar panel housing already has a battery, a charging panel and a weatherproof box, so we swap the light's circuit board for a RAK WisBlock board (RAK4631 core on a RAK19007 base) and mount an Alfa 5 dBi antenna on a 3D-printed arm.

The RAK board runs on Nordic's low-power nRF52840 chip, so the small panel is enough to keep it charged. It takes an afternoon, some basic soldering and about $97 in parts. The step-by-step photos below walk through the whole build.

ProcessorNordic nRF52840 on the RAK4631 core (Bluetooth LE 5.0)
RadioSemtech SX1262 LoRa, 902-928 MHz (US)

Role in the mesh

Where to buy and what it costs

There's no kit for this build. The light comes from Lowe's, the screws and nuts from Home Depot, and the rest from Amazon. Everything is linked in the parts list below. Make sure the RAK kit is the 915 MHz (US) version. You'll also need to 3D print the antenna arm and mount.

The build

The light's own circuit board and switch come out, and the RAK board takes their place, wired to the light's battery and solar panel with JST pigtails. The antenna cable runs out through a 3D-printed mounting arm to the Alfa antenna on top. You'll need a soldering iron, wire strippers, a screwdriver and access to a 3D printer.

Print the mounts in ABS or ASA. They live outside in direct sun. PLA softens in summer heat and breaks down in sunlight, while ABS and ASA handle both. ASA is the most UV-resistant.

Step by step

  1. Step 1 The solar light

    Get your spotlight and prepare your workspace.

    Flash the board first. Follow the Setup steps below before you install the board, so you don't have to reopen the enclosure later.

  2. Step 2 Separating the panel

    Unscrew the joint between the panel and the light all the way. Keep the screw for now.

  3. Step 3 Removing the back screws

    Separate the light and the panel as far as the cable will allow. Remove the four screws on the back corners of the panel.

  4. Step 4 Opening the panel

    Open the solar panel to expose the internals.

  5. Step 5 Removing the stock battery

    Remove the battery and set it aside.

  6. Step 6 Removing the switch

    Unscrew the switch panel, then cut the wires that lead to it as close to the board as possible to give you more room to work. You can throw this board away, but keep the screws. You'll use them to mount the RAK board in step 13.

  7. Step 7 Soldering the battery leads

    Strip the red and black battery leads and solder them to your JST connectors or pigtails.

    Check the polarity. Many JST pigtails come wired backwards, so don't go by the wire colors alone. Look at the RAK board and make sure the battery's red lead goes to the pin marked +. Getting this wrong will damage the board.

  8. Step 8 Soldering the solar leads

    Strip the black and white solar leads and solder them to your JST connectors or pigtails: black to black, and white to red.

    Check the polarity. The panel's white lead is positive. Make sure it goes to the pin marked + on the RAK board's solar connector. Getting this wrong will damage the board.

  9. Step 9 Routing the antenna: outside

    Identify the hole underneath the mounting area for your antenna to run.

  10. Step 10 Routing the antenna: inside

    Identify the hole in the top of the panel enclosure for your antenna to run.

  11. Step 11 Running the antenna cable: outside

    Run the antenna cable through the hole in the mounting area.

  12. Step 12 Running the antenna cable: inside

    Run the antenna cable through the hole in the top of the panel enclosure.

  13. Step 13 Mounting the RAK board

    Using the screws that held the switch board in place, attach the RAK board to the inside of the enclosure.

    Recommended (not pictured). Glue the silicone switch cover back into place to seal the hole and keep the weather out. We used superglue.

  14. Step 14 Mounting hardware

    To attach the 3D-printed mounting arm to the shell, we recommend an M4 machine screw and an M4 nylock nut.

  15. Step 15 Mounting the antenna arm

    Attach the 3D-printed mounting arm to the enclosure using the M4 machine screw and nylock nut from the previous step.

  16. Step 16 Connecting the antenna cable

    Attach the antenna cable (routed through the mounting eye) to the board. Press firmly and make sure it doesn't pop back out when you let go.

    Seal the cable hole. Now is the time to seal the hole the antenna cable runs through. We recommend silicone sealant or caulk, but hot glue or superglue will also work.

  17. Step 17 Attaching the antenna

    Attach the antenna to the connector on the back of the board.

    Antenna first. Attach the antenna before you connect the battery or the solar panel. Powering the board without an antenna can permanently damage it.

  18. Step 18 Connecting the battery

    Insert the battery into the holder, then plug the JST connector into the battery side of the board.

  19. Step 19 Connecting the solar panel

    Plug the JST connector from the solar panel into the solar side of the board.

    Not flashed yet? If the board isn't flashed and configured yet, do it now (see Setup), before you close the enclosure.

  20. Step 20 Closing the enclosure

    Fit the solar panel back onto the top of the enclosure with the original screws. Turn them slowly, as they can strip if you go too fast.

    Don't pinch any wires. A pinched wire can damage the board or the battery.

  21. Step 21 Finished

    Done! Enjoy your new mesh repeater.

The finished repeater

Setup

Connect the antenna before you power it on. Powering a LoRa radio without an antenna attached, even just over USB to flash it, can permanently damage it.

Flash the RAK board before you install it (see step 1). Plug it in over USB-C and use a web flasher (Chrome or Edge), choosing the RAK4631 firmware. If the flasher can't find it, double-press the reset button to put it in bootloader mode and try again. Then follow our recommended repeater settings.

  • Meshtastic: Meshtastic web flasher, then see Meshtastic infrastructure settings.
