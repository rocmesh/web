Heltec T114

A small, very low-power LoRa board that makes a great pocket companion for MeshCore or Meshtastic, with battery life measured in days rather than hours.

MeshCore Meshtastic Companion Low power ~$20-35

What it is

The Heltec Mesh Node T114 is a LoRa development board built around Nordic's nRF52840 chip and a Semtech SX1262 radio. The nRF52840 sips power compared to ESP32-based boards, so a T114 on a modest battery can run for days. It connects to your phone over Bluetooth, and you use the MeshCore or Meshtastic app to send and read messages.

It's sold as a bare board, optionally with a small 1.14" color screen and a GPS module. You add a battery, an antenna and a case, with suggestions linked below.

Processor Nordic nRF52840 (Bluetooth LE)
Radio Semtech SX1262 LoRa, 915 MHz (US)
Screen Optional 1.14" color TFT
GPS Optional add-on module
Antenna connector IPEX (u.FL), so you need a pigtail adapter for an SMA antenna
Power USB-C, or a 3.7 V LiPo battery with onboard charging
Firmware MeshCore, Meshtastic

Role in the mesh

Recommended

Companion

What it does best. Pair it with your phone over Bluetooth and carry it with you. The low power draw means you can leave it on all day.

Works

Repeater

Its low power use suits a small solar repeater. For a fixed site, though, a purpose-built repeater with a better antenna will cover much more ground. See the Basic Repeater.

Not recommended

Wi-Fi bridge

The nRF52840 has no Wi-Fi. If you need a node that talks to the internet, use an ESP32 board instead.

Assembly notes

  1. Flash the firmware before putting it in the case (see Setup below).
  2. Connect the pigtail to the board's IPEX connector. Press straight down until it clicks.
  3. Mount the SMA end in the case and screw on the antenna. Never power the radio without an antenna attached.
  4. Check the battery polarity, plug it in, and close up the case.
