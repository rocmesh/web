Heltec ESP32V3

A cheap, popular LoRa board with a built-in screen and Wi-Fi. It's a flexible starting point for almost any MeshCore or Meshtastic build.

MeshCore Meshtastic Home base Wi-Fi ~$18-30

What it is

The Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V3, usually just called the "Heltec V3", is one of the most common boards on the mesh. It pairs an ESP32-S3 chip, which has Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, with a Semtech SX1262 LoRa radio and a small 0.96" OLED screen. You can read messages right on the screen, or connect from the MeshCore or Meshtastic app over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi.

It's sold as a bare board. You add an antenna, a case, and if you want to carry it, a battery. Because it's cheap and well supported, it's the board most people use for custom builds, from handhelds to home base stations.

ProcessorESP32-S3 (Wi-Fi and Bluetooth LE 5.0)
RadioSemtech SX1262 LoRa, 902-928 MHz (US)
Screen0.96" OLED

Role in the mesh

Recommended

Home base companion

Plug it into USB power near a window and leave it on. The screen shows new messages at a glance, and Wi-Fi lets you connect from your computer as well as your phone.

Works

Pocket companion

With a battery and a case it makes a capable handheld, and there are countless case designs and builds to choose from. Expect to charge it daily, though: the ESP32 drains batteries much faster than nRF52 boards. For multi-day battery life, see the Heltec T114.

Not recommended

Solar repeater

It runs on solar, but the ESP32 needs a much bigger panel and battery than nRF52 boards to stay up through cloudy days. Fine as an indoor repeater on USB power. For an outdoor site, use the Basic Repeater.

Where to buy and what it costs

Always buy the 915 MHz version for use in the US. Heltec makes a version for each region, and the others won't work legally or well here.

Setup

Flash the firmware from a web browser (Chrome or Edge) with the Heltec V3 plugged in over USB-C, then follow our recommended settings. In Meshtastic, turning on Wi-Fi turns off Bluetooth, so pick whichever you'll connect with.
